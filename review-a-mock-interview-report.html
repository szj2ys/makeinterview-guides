How to review a mock interview report without overfitting

A report is useful when it changes the next answer. It becomes a trap when the goal shifts from improving the decision to reproducing a model sentence word for word.

Separate three signals

  1. Structure: did the main point arrive before the supporting detail?
  2. Evidence: is there a specific action, constraint, metric, or observable result?
  3. Delivery: where did filler, hedging, or a buried conclusion change how the answer sounded?

Choose one fix per session

Mark the single sentence that cost the answer the most clarity. Rewrite that sentence, then answer the same question again without reading the full report. A second attempt tests whether the correction transferred into spontaneous speech.

Keep the evidence, not the wording

Model answers are useful for locating missing constraints or outcomes. Your final wording should still sound like something you could say naturally under pressure. Reuse the structure and the facts; do not memorize phrases that collapse when the question changes.

Start with a sample interview report and mark the first answer whose main point arrived too late. Note only the structural change you would make.
