How to answer a career-gap question

Give a direct, proportionate explanation and reconnect the gap to the role without apology or invention.

What the interviewer is listening for

The interviewer is not looking for a polished speech. They are checking whether you can choose the right evidence, explain a decision you controlled, and keep the facts consistent when the next question makes the situation less comfortable.

State the dates and factual reason in one or two sentences.

Build the answer in five moves

  1. Set the question. Restate the real prompt—“Can you explain the gap in your résumé?”—in your own words and name the decision you need to explain.
  2. Use one example. Select a situation where you can distinguish your contribution from the team’s and where the outcome can be checked.
  3. Show the evidence. Describe what you did during the gap only when it affects readiness, availability, or the role’s requirements.
  4. Make the decision visible. Explain how you decided the timing was right to return or move into this work.
  5. Land the result, then stop. Close with current evidence of readiness and invite the interviewer to ask what matters.
Worked rehearsal

“I was away from full-time work from January through July for a family care responsibility. I kept one small technical project active for continuity, but I do not want to overstate it as a second career. I returned because the care plan is now stable and my availability is predictable. In the last month I completed the same system-design review process I use for production work and narrowed the type of role I am seeking. I’m happy to discuss any readiness concern directly.”

Follow-up drill

After saying the answer once without notes, ask these questions out loud. The goal is to add evidence without inventing a new story:

  • What would you do if a similar constraint returned?
  • Why did you choose this role after the gap?
  • Which skill needs the most rebuilding?

Common failure to avoid

Do not invent consulting work, conceal the timeline, or over-explain private information. Match the explanation to the role’s actual concern.

If the interviewer challenges a fact, separate what you owned from what the team owned. Correct an overstated claim immediately. A smaller, accurate result survives follow-up questions better than a larger result that cannot be explained.

Self-check before you record

  • The timeline is consistent with the résumé.
  • The reason is stated at a proportionate level of detail.
  • Any activity is described accurately.
  • Readiness evidence is recent and relevant.
  • You do not pressure the interviewer to accept a story they cannot verify.
